# Zendesk support audit: common asks and human resolution steps

**Window:** 2026-08-02 to 2026-10-01 (four ~2-week passes) | **Source:** Zendesk tickets as indexed in Glean (read-only) | **Scope:** all products; tickets tagged `group_support*` (L1/L2/L3, `group_sla`).

## 1. Coverage and caveats (read first)

- **This is a large sample, not a full export.** About 600+ distinct tickets surfaced across the four windows; roughly 340 were read in full or in part (about 40 / 76 / 95 / 105 per window). The rest were classified by subject and tags.
- **No `group_cs*` tag exists in the index.** No ticket carried one. CS hand-offs ("CS Followup ticket from Support ticket #…") carry no group tag or a TAM group. They are included as a separate category because their body is the support engineer's summary of steps already taken. Drop them if you want a strict tag filter.
- **Resolutions are mostly not visible.** Glean often indexes only the first 2-20 comments of a ticket. Where no resolution was visible it is recorded as "not visible", not inferred. Attachments, Slack threads, Jira content and call content are not indexed.
- **Excluded:** bot-created tickets with no human action and no group tag (about 100+, e.g. 170273 production DB pending and 169725 cluster loss were flagged as untriaged and urgent), AP/finance/procurement mail, marketing, recruiting, Forethought chatbot-only tickets.
- **Safety:** one ticket (169210) contained a script probe and text addressed to an AI; it was treated as hostile spam and ignored. No credentials, customer emails or meeting passcodes are reproduced here.

## 2. Use cases: common asks and the steps humans take

### A. Production outage, replication, sync and CRDB / Replica-Of failures (Redis Software, K8s)
Examples: 171535, 171838, 171681, 171387, 171265, 171446, 168968, 170280, 171049.
- **Typical ask:** join an urgent bridge; explain a down cluster, a replication link that stays down after node recovery, or a stuck sync.
- **Steps:**
  1. Acknowledge within minutes (auto-reply, then a human).
  2. Send the support-package (SP) upload link. For CRDB / Replica-Of, ask for an SP from **every participating cluster**.
  3. Tag `@l3_to_review` / `@supportrl!` with the SLA tier and a short summary; ticket moves to `group_support_l3`.
  4. Analyzer bot posts an SP health summary; L2/L3 reads the SP and marks `@l3_review_done`.
  5. If the customer goes quiet: reminders at about days 2-3, 4-5 and 7, then a no-response close.
- **Visible resolutions:** rare. Flex outage 171535 recovered by restarting the K8s liveness/readiness probes; 170280 L3 noted "ping doesn't prove TCP" and a CS ticket was opened for Professional Services.

### B. Cloud connectivity, timeouts, failovers and "database stuck" states
Examples: 172455, 171921, 168971, 168685, 168496, 169737, 170200, 168154, 170261.
- **Typical ask:** timeouts, READONLY/LOADING errors after a failover, a database stuck Pending or active-change-pending, or "can't connect after maintenance".
- **Steps:**
  1. Ask for endpoint, DB IDs, client name and version, stack trace with timezone, and whether `redis-cli` works.
  2. Check the SLA tier (Basic SLA gets normal priority even if the bot set Urgent; explain this to the customer).
  3. Post the account template (SLA, subscription, BDB, endpoints, CMDB, Grafana) and check Grafana / Loki / cluster events (for example an instance failure with no maintenance that day).
  4. Escalate to L3; DevOps pulls a cluster-level SP when the BO-generated one is incomplete.
  5. For stuck state machines (invalid ACL pattern, 168154 / 170200): customer fixes the ACL, then DevOps is tagged to unstick the database.
- **Visible resolutions:** 169737 (READONLY traced to a watchdog failover; Jira filed; endpoint still pointed at the old node); 168685 (DB found empty after maintenance, root cause not stated).

### C. Cloud-ops proactive alerts (largest group, about 100 tickets across the window)
Examples: throughput over limit (170158, 169814, 170480, 171275), node bandwidth over 95% (171539, 170544, 171297), connections near limit (171629, 168341), uneven shards (168864, 171270), node replacement and failovers (169261, 168420), shard crashes (171482, 172189), maintenance overruns (169049, 169733).
- **Typical ask:** none from the customer; Cloud Ops opens the ticket with Grafana evidence.
- **Steps:**
  1. Support L1 copies the original subject and pastes the account template.
  2. L1 drafts the customer message (often tagged for L3 review: "LGTM, proceed"). Advice is usually: raise throughput/memory limits, rebalance shards, replace `KEYS` with `SCAN`, tune FT.SEARCH, split maintenance windows.
  3. If there is no reply after about two attempts, hand off to CS/TAM via a "CS Followup" ticket with a structured "what is the issue / current status / why moving to CS / next step" summary.
  4. Basic-SLA customers get priority lowered to normal.
- **Known root causes seen:** slow Lua or long `KEYS` blocking a shard (170285, 169130, 172295); disk-busy AOF fsync (172189); FT.AGGREGATE crashes (171482); AWS throttling on bandwidth (171297); proxy policy `single` with dense shard placement causing 30-37 s outages per reboot (170577, recommendation: all-nodes proxy policy).
- **Customer replies are rare**; outcomes mostly "informed customer / not visible".

### D. Upgrades, maintenance exclusions, proactive events and standby support
Examples: 172148, 171624, 172489, 170945, 169573, 167075, 166998.
- **Typical ask:** a failed or stuck upgrade ("Change Pending"), a maintenance exclusion, a dedicated bridge for a launch or sale event.
- **Steps:** confirm 24x7 coverage and the "reply urgent" keyword; collect the event window, time zone, databases and scenarios; L2 runs a SOP health check on SPs ahead of time (flags such as `KEYS` in slowlogs, fsync-every-write); name a standby engineer; check in after the window and close. Maintenance exclusions go on a form and DevOps approves. Support does not do step-by-step proactive joining; Professional Services can, for a fee.
- **Visible outcome:** 170945 event passed with no issues.

### E. Redis Software on Kubernetes / OpenShift: setup, monitoring, SAML, recovery after patching
Examples: 166960, 168186, 171120, 169653, 169284, 170021.
- **Steps:** request `log_collector` output (set `--k8s_cli` if kubectl is not found) and an SP; L3 identifies known issues (RED-173258: BDBs stuck in FINAL ERROR; recovery via `rlutil reset_bdb_sm` resume); for monitoring, supply the ServiceMonitor YAML and Grafana dashboard repo, noting licence metrics exist since 7.22.0-216.
- **Expected-behavior answer:** 169653, pdns is intentionally off on Kubernetes deployments.

### F. SSO, login and account access
Examples: 168135, 170060, 169445, 172028, 172361.
- **Steps:** verify the SSO test connection; for a SAML lockout after certificate renewal, convert the user to a local user, log in, then update the SAML config (168135). For role-mapping errors, request the IdP config and a HAR capture and find the error code (`saml-config-invalid-account-mapping`, 170060); L3 reviews the draft reply. Lost MFA and social-login recovery are routed to the cloud team. Password resets are sent by the engineer and confirmed in Kibana.

### G. Billing, subscriptions, cancellations and licences
Examples: 172387, 171631, 171536, 170811, 170904, 168966, 169133, 3M licensing tickets.
- **Steps:** acknowledge; pull Service Manager logs and subscription history (creation/deletion dates, payment-method removal); explain billing policy where clear; route to Billing or Cloud ("could you please assist"). Blocked accounts are unblocked via procedure once payment is evidenced (172387). Cancellations: engineer asks the cloud team to delete the database and subscription. Expired licences go to CS/TAM for renewal.
- **Resolutions mostly not visible** except 172387.

### H. Data loss and recovery requests
Examples: 170977, 172559, 171446.
- **Steps:** collect Service Manager logs and deleted-database info and tag L3 "DB Recovery/Data Loss". 170977: automated deprovisioning of unpaid Vercel marketplace resources. 171446: restore returned 39k-156k of 266k keys; suspected TTL expiry or clock skew; the customer never answered.

### I. RDI, modules, search
Examples: 170938, 167902, 169893, 169370.
- **Steps:** L3 and R&D are looped in at once if a live call is under way. 170938: regression from a manually deployed internal build; **workaround: delete and recreate the UAT pipeline**. 167902: SP, RDI version and config.yaml, connectivity checks to the RDI database endpoint, RDI expert call; blocked by customer inactivity.

### J. Misrouted or non-technical
Certification exams and Redis University (redirect to university@), HR verification, marketing, vendor mail, internal customer threads ("looks internal", set pending, close).

## 3. Cross-cutting patterns

1. **The first human step is almost always acknowledgement plus an information or SP request.** Resolution rarely happens at L1; L1's job is intake, the account template, and escalation by tagging the L3 queue.
2. **Email-opened tickets are auto-closed** (`closed_email_not_allowed`, `no_resolution`) and an engineer re-files a follow-up ticket pointing the customer to the portal; duplicates are merged. This fragments threads (e.g. 171263 into 171265, 168967 into 168968, 170935 into 170631).
3. **Customer non-response after the SP request is the most common ending.** Reminder cadence is about days 2, 4 and 7, then a closure macro.
4. **Priority/SLA mismatches are common:** the bot or customer sets Urgent while the SLA is Basic; engineers lower priority and explain.
5. **Hand-off to CS/TAM** happens for throughput/usage guidance, renewals, sizing and customers who do not reply to two outreach attempts.
6. **Recurring root causes worth automating or documenting:** throughput over provisioned limit; `KEYS` / slow Lua blocking shards; uneven shard distribution; proxy policy and maintenance-window overruns on large shards; SAML lockout after certificate rotation; ACL patterns that stick a database in change-pending; K8s `log_collector` kubectl path.

## 4. Candidate knowledge-base / macro opportunities

- "Throughput over limit" customer notice with resize/optimize options and a CS hand-off criterion.
- CRDB / Replica-Of sync-down intake macro that asks for an SP from all participating clusters up front.
- SAML lockout recovery after certificate renewal (local-user workaround).
- `KEYS` to `SCAN` guidance, plus a CRDB-safe SCAN+TTL script (167721).
- K8s `log_collector` steps including `--k8s_cli`.
- Pre-event standby checklist (what to send, what L2 checks in the SP).
- Billing: marketplace deprovisioning after unpaid invoices (Vercel) and account-unblock procedure.
